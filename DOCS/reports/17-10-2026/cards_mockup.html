Task cards: three directions

Mockups for requirement 2 (cards in every view, status stands out) and requirement 3 (wider Week, hide weekends). Colors, font and spacing come straight from src/App.css and the TasksView CSS modules. Monet ships a single dark theme, so there is no light version here. The data mirrors real usage: short titles that only make sense with project and group, a due date on almost every task, rescheduling, checklists and the custom Waiting status.

B

Status block

a solid, square-cornered status tag that opens the card, due date on the right

The card opens with a solid status block flush to the top-left corner, with no rounding, carrying the time in status. The due date, the one date set on every task, sits on the right of the same line. Custom fields get their own key/value area below a hairline.

RationaleFollows the brief literally: a status tag with square corners. The solid fill makes it the one loud element in the card. Everything else stays muted, and the due date sits next to it because it is the other thing you check first.

C

Status field

the whole card is tinted with the status color, properties as a mini table

The status colors the whole card surface with a faint tint and a 2px top rule. Project and group sit above the title like a path. Dates, checklist and custom fields share one aligned property list, so built-in and custom metadata look the same.

List · one row per task
RationaleStatus as an area rather than a label: in Week, a day full of blue cards tells you it is in progress at a glance. The property list gives custom metadata a natural slot with no special styling.
Reads firstThe card tint (status), then project / group, then the title. The property labels slow down reading a little but make every value unambiguous.
High densityThe weakest. The property table adds 2 to 4 lines per card, and List rows with alternating tints look striped and busy.
RisksTints shift the text contrast for each status and become muddy on the dark background. To do grey barely tints at all. It needs per-status contrast checks and ignores the brief's bubble idea.

Week: wider columns, hide weekends

cards in variant B, compact mode (the column already shows the scheduled date)

Today each column has a minimum of 168px, with 8 columns (Overdue plus 7 days). Proposal: a minimum of 200px, which with weekends hidden leaves Mon–Fri at roughly 1.5× today's width. The Weekends toggle sits in the week nav next to the week arrows and is saved as a preference. With weekends hidden, Saturday and Sunday shrink into a single 36px rail that shows how many tasks are hidden. Clicking the rail shows the weekend again, so nothing disappears silently.

Weekends visible

Weekends hidden: Mon–Fri take the freed space, the rail shows 2 hidden tasks

Edge cases: when today is a Saturday or Sunday, show the weekend columns that week even if the preference says hide. While dragging, the rail is a valid drop target: holding a card over it for about 400ms expands the weekend.

Recommendation: B

B is the only option that gives status a dedicated, recognizable element in every view, and it does so the way the brief asked: a tag with square corners. It also puts the due date, present on 100% of tasks, right next to it. A is the safe fallback if density wins. C looks good in Week but costs too much space and contrast.

  • Kanban: the column already names the status, so the block shrinks to a 7px square of the status color plus the time in status (3d). Keep the full block in List, Week, Today, Projects, Groups and Review.
  • Custom fields: show at most 3 key/value rows in the card, then +N more. In List, show the first 2 inline. Checkbox values appear as ✓/–. Hide fields with no value.
  • Rare fields (important, recurrence, Plan today) stay as small icons in the footer, never in the top line.
  • Built-in statuses have color = null. Use the existing --status-* tokens as the block fill, and use dark text (#161625) on every fill for contrast. A custom status with a null color falls back to --text-muted.
  • Selection keeps the app's orange (--accent-task) as a 1px border. Because Waiting is also orange, consider a warning when someone picks a status color close to the accent.
